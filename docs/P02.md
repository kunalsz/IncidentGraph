# P02 : RANK INIT PHASE

Where P1 builds the evidence picture, P2 decides who the suspects are: it scores every service in the P1 scope and picks the branches that P3's agents will actually investigate.
It is "deterministic before probabilistic": every graph-computable score is calculated before any model sees the incident, so the LLM adjudicates a shortlist rather than ranking from scratch. P2 reads only from the immutable ScopeResult snapshot , it never queries the graph to rank.

## What it does
The pure core is `rank_candidates()` and the phase entry point `rank_investigation()` adds the guarded checks (must be a session graph, scope must match the investigation's incident/anchor/window) and writes one artifact.

It computes five component scores per service:

| Component | What it measures | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| phi1 | Instability: empirical p99/p50 of the service's own span latencies | latency_scores, scorers.py:16 |
| phi3 | Impact: total latency × max(0, 1 − slowest_callee_avg/own_avg) , the self-processing share, so a caller that's just waiting on a slow child scores low | same |
| severity | max(sev_m, sev_l, sev_tc) carried straight from P1 | p2_rank.py:32 |
| rwr | Anchor-rooted random walk over DEPENDS_ON, following caller→dependency (cause-seeking), dangling mass restarting at the anchor, φ=0.15, 20 iterations | random_walk, scorers.py:44 |
| deploy_proximity | exp(−Δhours/24) since the last change , again carried from P1 | p2_rank.py:33 |

Those five are combined by reciprocal rank fusion (`scorers.py:66`): each component votes with 1/(ε + rank), ε=60, ties share their average rank, and services scoring zero on a component simply don't vote: missing signals contribute nothing rather than a fake penalty. The fused list is then ordered by score descending, with the node's pagerank property as the exact-tie breaker, then id.

The output split matters: the top 8 (TOP_K_CANDIDATES) become P3 branches, but so does every rule-flagged service , anything with a self-time flag, an n-σ hit, or error logs is admitted regardless of rank. Everything ranked below the cutoff without a flag goes into an audit list with the reason outside_top_k_without_rule_flag, which later becomes the report's "ruled out" section. So the shortlist can grow past 8 when the rules demand it, but never silently shrinks.

There are three policies: `baseline_rrf` (anchored walk; the product default),
`severity_only`, and `rrf_anchor_neutral` (uniform-restart walk, for cold ranking
where the anchor is not case-derived). `validate_rank_policy` rejects anything
else. Which one runs is declared by the caller and recorded in the ranking
config: the engine defaults to `baseline_rrf`, the RCAEval harness to
`severity_only`. See `output/replication-2026-10-19/RESULTS.md` for the
two-corpus comparison behind that split.

## What P2 deliberately does not do

- **Gamma is evidence, not a vote.** The indicator gamma scores are persisted
  with the ranking and shown in the component table, but they are not an extra
  fusion component; the five-component fusion above is the whole ranking.
- **No correlation signal is used.** Correlation pruning is disabled because
  neither the authored fixtures nor the benchmark adapters provide aligned raw
  time series, so no correlation-based causal claim is made anywhere in the
  product. A replayed ranking reports `correlation_pruning:
  "unavailable_without_aligned_raw_series"` rather than an empty result.
- **Branch selection is not the ranking.** The ranking orders every candidate;
  which services actually get investigated is a separate, caller-declared
  choice: the engine keeps every rule-flagged service, the benchmark harness
  narrows the initial branches to a three-service coverage floor with one
  hypothesis slot each.
